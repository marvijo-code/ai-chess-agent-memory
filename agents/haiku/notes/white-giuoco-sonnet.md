# White Giuoco Pianissimo v Sonnet 5.5 (T44R2, L m32)

Lines I played (each under 16 plies):
- 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.O-O Nf6 5.d3 d6 6.c3 O-O (12 plies)
- 7.d4 exd4 8.Nxd4 Nxd4 9.cxd4 Bb6 10.Nc3 Bg4 11.Qd2 Re8 (10 plies)

These were fine: the d4 break, Nxd4 Nxd4 cxd4, Qd2 forced by Bg4 hitting Qd1, Re1 adding a second guard to e4 against Nf6 and Re8, and h3 kicking Bg4. Level to m18.

Failures:
1. 14.Qe3?! put the queen on e3 with my e4 pawn between it and Re8. That pawn was the only screen. Black's ...d5 break made it a loaded line.
2. 16.Nd5?! let ...c6 kick the knight. 17.Nxb6 Nxb6 18.Bd3 was acceptable; 18...d5 was the break.
3. 19.exd5?? Rxe3 20.Rxe3 Bxd3 21.Rxd3 Qxd5: queen for rook. The queen had to leave the e-file or the screen had to stay before any pawn exchange. No verified queen move stored; work it out on the board.
4. Rook on d1 was my only rank-1 defender. 26.a4?? removed a2's guard of b3, so 26...Qxb3 forked Rd1 and Bc3.
5. 27.Be1? was pinned on the first rank (Qd1 vs Kg1, f1 empty). 27...Qxd1 won the rook and 28...Qxe1 the bishop.
6. 29.Kg3 and 30.Kf4 walked the king toward Ne4+. 32.Kf5 Qe4# was mate with Nf2 guarding e4.

Next time (unverified, check each on the board):
- Do not put the queen behind a pawn that an enemy rook already faces on the same file.
- Before a pawn exchange that removes a screen, list the line it opens and what sits at the far end.
- Before a pawn push, list what that pawn guarded and what his queen takes next.
- Keep the last rank-1 rook at home; move a bishop only after a pin check.
- With his queen and knight near my king, prefer blocks and piece trades over king walks.
