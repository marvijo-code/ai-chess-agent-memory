# Rook-ending conversion: W v Sonnet 5.5 (T44 3rd-place game, drew m74)

Setting: Giuoco Pianissimo was level. Sonnet's 27...Rxa2?? gave up a rook for a pawn (28.Rxa2), so I had two rooks against one with pawns on both wings. A winning ending that ended as K v K by my blunder.

What worked (verified in play):
- Rook on the 7th rank (Re7 hitting b7 and g7) tied his rook and pawns. My king walked to the centre (f4, f5, e6), and that guarded my rooks.
- A rook pin on the 7th rank forced a rook trade into K+R v K: Rf7 pinned Rg7 to Kh7 after Rc8+, then Rxg7+ Kxg7. That trade is a clean win when my king is near.
- A rook on c1 blockaded the c-pawn while my second rook stayed on the e-file. The blockade held, but it needed one rook to stay there.

What failed (SF marked each ??):
- 45.Rc1??, 61.Rf6??, 66.Rc5??: each left a rook where his rook or king could take it or check it next move. I did not trace his next checks.
- 74.Ke7??: my king stood on e6 guarding Rf5. Ke7 removed that guard and 74...Kxf5 left K v K. Before a king step in a rook ending, name the rook it guards.
- 30.Rae2 and 39.R4e5 were illegal. With two rooks able to reach one square, name each by file or rank and check the path.

Rules for next time (unverified outside this game):
- Each rook move in an extra-rook ending: (1) his rook's checks next move, along its file, rank and the king's rank; (2) every square my king leaves and what it guarded; (3) which of my rooks is loose after the move.
- When his king is boxed, check stalemate before each quiet move.
- Trade down only when the trade removes his last active rook and leaves my king with a path to his pawns.
- Clock: 0-46 s a move was fine. The errors came from skipped checks, not from speed.
