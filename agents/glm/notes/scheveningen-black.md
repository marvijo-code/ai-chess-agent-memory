# Scheveningen/Taimanov order as Black vs 5.Qxd4 (notes)

## T38 R5 v SF19, L m30 (900+10)
Line: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.Nc3 a6 4.d4 cxd4 5.Qxd4 Nc6 6.Qe3 Be7 7.Be2 d6 8.O-O Nf6 (16 plies); then 9.Na4 O-O 10.h3 Qc7 11.c4 b5! 12.cxb5 axb5! 13.Bxb5 Bd7 14.Nb6 15.Qxb6 (14 plies).
- The good phase: ...b5! hit his loose Na4 AND loose c4-pawn at once; SF had to concede a pawn. One pawn push hitting TWO loose units is the best Black resource in this line - look for it before routine development.
- 13...Bd7?! attacked the loose Bb5 but left Bd7 AND Ra8 loose TOGETHER: 14.Nb6! fork. I had verified only that Nc6 blocked the b5-d7 pin - never asked what the arriving knight hits on its other 6 squares. Rule: a knight jump that is BLOCKED has one target; ask which TWO of my pieces are loose on its jumps.
- THE BLUNDER, 14...Qxb6?? 'even queen trade': his QUEEN stood on e3; b6 held the knight that had JUST arrived. Q for N, lost. The occupant of a square he just moved to is the new piece - re-read before any capture.
- COUNT COLLAPSE: after 15.Qxb6 my notes held 'even, down 1 pawn', then 'down Q for N+P', then 'up a full piece' (the EVEN 17.Bxd7 Nxd7 I misread as winning a bishop), then 'down Q for P', then 'down Q+B for P'. Four claims in one game. Fix: full piece list W:/B: after every capture, never a delta.
- Survivable play while down: 20...Ne5 (d6-guarded), 26...Rd8 guarding d6 vs Nf5, rooks kept behind the king squares. 21...Kf8 attacked Ne7 but 22.Qe3 guarded it - count HIS guards on the square before a king capture.
- THE END, 28...gxh6??: an 'even pawn trade' that REMOVED g7's pawn guard. His Qe3 + Nf5: 29.Qxh6+ Kg8 30.Qg7#. Before capturing any pawn adjacent to my castled king, name what it stops guarding and whether his queen reaches that square. Better: let h6 stand, keep f7/g7/h7 shell, look for ...d5/...Qd7-...Re7 counterplay instead.
- SF's h4-h5-h6 push vs my castled shell recurs here; ...g6 is suicide vs his Q+B/N battery, so the answer must be prepared BEFORE h6 lands (queen trade done correctly, or rooks covering f8/g8 flights).
- One illegal O-O try at move 7 (Ng8 blocked f8): castle only after listing knight + both squares.

## T36 R1 v SF19, L m29
Line: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.Nc3 a6 4.d4 cxd4 5.Qxd4 Nc6 6.Qe3 Nf6 7.e5 Nd5 8.Nxd5 exd5 (16 plies); then 9.Bd2 Be7 10.c4 O-O?? 11.cxd5.
- Opening comfortable (his Qxd4/Qe3 retreat costs tempo). Execution failures killed it.
- 11...Qb6?? double error: (1) a sweep that listed PAWN attackers but never traced HIS queen's own diagonal e3-d4-c5-b6; (2) 'defended' d5 with Nc6 - c6 hits a5/b4/d4/e5/e7/a7/b8/d8, never d5. 11.cxd5 won a pawn. vs 10.c4 I need a REAL second guard of d5 (...Qd7) or ...b5 counterplay; castling does not resolve tension. While his Q sits on e3 the whole e3-a7 diagonal is hers: nothing of mine on b6/c5/a7 without square-by-square tracing.
- 16...Be5?? 'defended by Bf6': Bf6 WAS the bishop moving - the mover never defends its own destination. Rule: when listing defenders of a destination, EXCLUDE the moving piece.
- 23...g6?? self-mate: my g7-pawn guarded h6; after ...g6 his Qe3+Bg2 battery played Qxh6+ Kg8 Qg6+ ... Qh7#. Never push g6 (or move off f7) vs Q+B battery on my castled king; trade his bishop first.
- One illegal try (axb6 - a6-pawn captures forward to b5 only). Repetition fantasy at m28 while down Q+B: down huge vs SF, the only resource is keeping the queen.
