## Key lessons (check before EVERY move)
- QUEEN DESTINATION SWEEP - PAWNS AND SLIDERS BOTH (fatal 4x): before ANY queen move - quiet, capture or CHECK - list (1) every enemy PAWN attacking the destination (each covers 2 squares: e3 <- d2/f2) AND (2) every enemy SLIDER's line traced square by square. The a2-g8 diagonal has now eaten my queen (T36: his Qe3, e3-d4-c5-b6 all empty - my note literally said 'no attacker on b6'). A check answered by a pawn capture = Q for P; a queen landing on a clear enemy diagonal = Q for nothing. The planned follow-up is irrelevant if the landing square itself is capturable.
- KNIGHT GEOMETRY: recite the 8 squares before trusting any knight guard. c6: a5/a7/b4/b8/d4/d8/e5/e7 - NEVER d5 or b5 (T36: 'd5 defended by Nc6', 12.cxd5 won a clean pawn). f6: d5/e4/d7/e8/g4/g8/h5/h7 - never e5 (T34R4 17...Qxe5??).
- SELF-GUARD ILLUSION: the piece being MOVED never defends its own destination. 16...Be5?? was 'defended by Bf6' - Bf6 WAS the bishop moving; 17.Nxe5 free. When counting guards of a destination, EXCLUDE the mover.
- BLOCKED DEFENDER - before ANY capture or move onto a square 'defended' by my bishop/rook, walk the line SQUARE BY SQUARE and name each occupant. A guard through my own piece DOES NOT EXIST. Resolve the blocker or change the plan.
- KINGSHIELD PUSHES vs Q+B BATTERY: g7-pawn guards h6 AND f6. Before ...g6 (or any pawn move off g7/f7) while his queen + dark-square bishop aim at my castled king (Qe3/Qg4 + Bg2/Bd4/Bh6), name what the push stops guarding - ...g6 bared h6 and h4/h5 pressure gave Qxh6+ Kg8 Qxg6+ Kh8 Qxf6+ mate (T36). Shuffle solid or trade his bishop first.
- EVERY SAME-TYPE PIECE: with two enemy rooks (or bishops), name EACH one's current file/rank/diagonal before moving. 21.Rxa7?? - I checked Rb8 and Bd6 and forgot Ra8, the rook standing on the very file. When a piece escapes along a rank/file, the adjacent rook usually sees the landing square.
- ROOK ONTO A ROOK'S FILE: 24.Rb1?? walked onto Rb2's b-file with zero guards = last rook gone. Before any rook move, sweep the destination file AND rank for enemy rooks, then count my recapturers on the landing square.
- DESTINATION ATTACKERS: list every enemy piece attacking the destination and all my guards from real geometry. 20...Bd7?? (T35R3) had zero guards vs Rc7. Model: 24...Re8 guarded the attacked Be7.
- FINISH THE NAMED SEQUENCE: 'capture, then recapture' means the recapture is the MOVE PLAYED (T35R1 13.Rd1?? = piece lost; T35R3 Rxd8/Rxe7 worked). After HIS capture the first question is 'what recaptures'.
- CAPTURE DESTINATION: name MY defenders of the LANDING square from real geometry, excluding the mover itself.
- FORK REALITY: my check/fork is real only if nothing captures my piece on its destination - scan every slider's line first (8.Nc7+?? Qxc7). Verify HIS checks too: 37.Qh5+?? was undefended, Kxh5 won his queen.
- RECAPTURE FIRST / ONLY-QUEEN GUARD: after every trade re-count guards of attacked pieces; a piece whose only guard is my queen is lost when his second attacker lands (13...Bxd4, T34R3).
- ATTACKED PIECE CAPTURES FIRST: before a pawn attack on a piece, check what it captures en route (13.g5?? Bxd4, T34R3).
- MATE FIRST: after every his move ask what mates and what a pawn push OPENS (T36 23...g6 opened Qxh6+; T34R1 23.exf6 -> Qxg7#; 26...Qe1# back rank).
- DOWN MATERIAL: trade pieces, no grabs, blockade, shuffle - and vs SF expect NO repetition rescue once he is far ahead (T36: I tracked threefold while down Q+B for N; he just mated). The discipline executes well when applied AT the blunder moment, not after; it cannot rescue a Q-for-nothing hole.
- MATERIAL FROM THE BOARD: recount after every capture before choosing a plan. At T35R4 m15 I wrote 'one pawn down' while a full KNIGHT down - the fantasy count drove pawn grabs.
- SCAN each move: his last move's new attacks; checks and knight jumps; pawn breaks both ways; my loose pieces and their CURRENT guards. Re-run guard counts after every capture or king move.
- LEGALITY: destination empty and not mine, path empty, SAN fits; DOUBLED ROOKS need disambiguation (Rad8/Red8); castling needs both squares empty and home pieces moved; pawns attack DIAGONALLY FORWARD only (a6-pawn hits b5, never b6; his e7-pawn made Kf8 illegal). After ONE rejection redraw and pick a DIFFERENT idea.
- CLOCK: blunders, not pace, decide. A long think that skips the trace/recapture is worse than a fast one that plays it (T36: 30 s on Qb6, sweep still skipped sliders).
