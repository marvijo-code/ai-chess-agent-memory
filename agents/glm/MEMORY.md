## Key lessons (check before EVERY move)
- MY RECAPTURER BEFORE ANY RETREAT (fatal 15...Qe7??): after HIS capture the first question is 'what of MINE recaptures that destination' - name each of my pieces hitting it with its line traced (Qc7xc5 down the clear c-file). 'Nothing recaptures' must be a checked claim, never a feeling. Retreating instead of an equal recapture turns a trade into a gift.
- CAPTURE EXTENDS HIS RAYS: after ANY enemy capture, sweep the capturing piece's lines FROM ITS NEW SQUARE before moving anything near it (his Bxc5 owned d6 and e7 the instant it landed). Retreating onto a fresh diagonal of the piece that just captured = suicide.
- ATTACK A GUARDED PIECE = NO MOVE (fatal 14...Bc5??): count HIS defenders of the target first (his Be3 was guarded by Qd2). An attack must win on recapture arithmetic, not just 'hit' something.
- CAPTURE = FULL DESTINATION SWEEP (fatal 26...Rxc2+??): before taking, trace EVERY enemy slider's line to the destination square by square, plus pawns and knights. 'Is the target undefended' describes HIS guards of HIS piece - it does NOT say my piece survives there.
- PASSED PAWN'S PATH = ATTACK SQUARES (fatal 13...Bd7??): an enemy pawn captures on its two forward diagonals - never park a piece there, and before a grab elsewhere list what the passer eats next (Qxg5! was right, but the d5-passer then ate Nc6: a grab only pays when the passer's path is handled first).
- BISHOP GUARD = ONE CONTINUOUS DIAGONAL (fatal 33.Bd6??): name the full unbroken line; a bend = no guard. HIS bishop guards obey the same walk.
- UNGUARDED PIECES OUTLIVE THE PUSH (fatal 7.d3??): a pawn push that removes a guard leaves the hole until something REPLACES it. Every move: which of my pieces have NO current guard?
- ROOK LINES: (a) a rook attacks its FIRST blocker (38.Bd4?? Rxd4); (b) never walk my rook onto an enemy rook's file; (c) with two enemy rooks name EACH one's lines before moving (21.Rxa7?? forgot Ra8). Model: 41...Kxb7! - check if the blockading rook is guarded before it promotes the pawn.
- KNIGHTS GO ON THE SWEEP LIST (fatal 17.Bxe4??): attackers = PAWNS + SLIDERS + EVERY ENEMY KNIGHT (c5-knight: a4/a6/b3/b7/d3/d7/e4/e6).
- SHUFFLE = GUARD DUTY LOSS: before ANY move, shuffles included, name what the moved piece WAS guarding on the old board.
- QUEEN DESTINATION SWEEP (fatal 5x): before ANY queen move - quiet, capture or CHECK - list every enemy PAWN attacking the destination AND every enemy SLIDER's line traced square by square. A check answered by a pawn capture = Q for P.
- KNIGHT GEOMETRY: recite the 8 squares before trusting a knight guard. c6: a5/a7/b4/b8/d4/d8/e5/e7 - NEVER d5. f6: d5/e4/d7/e8/g4/g8/h5/h7 - never e5.
- CHECKS PASS THE SAME SWEEP (44.Rc8+?? Bxc8): full destination sweep (pawns, sliders, knights, king flights) before delivering ANY check.
- SELF-GUARD ILLUSION: the piece being MOVED never defends its own destination; exclude the mover from guard counts.
- BLOCKED DEFENDER: walk the guard line SQUARE BY SQUARE and name each occupant. A guard through any piece DOES NOT EXIST.
- ROOK-7TH = MATE NEXT (T36R5): luft ...h6 BEFORE shuffling when down material - first verify his battery does not punish it. MATE FIRST applies to rook endings too.
- KINGSHIELD PUSHES vs Q+B BATTERY: g7 guards h6 AND f6. Never push ...g6 while his queen + dark bishop aim at my king (T36R1). GOOD model: 13...Kh8 - then trade queens at first contact.
- FINISH THE NAMED SEQUENCE: the named recapture must BE the move played. GOOD: Rxd8/Rxe7, Kxf1!.
- FORK REALITY: my check/fork is real only if nothing captures my piece on its destination - scan every slider's line first (8.Nc7+?? Qxc7).
- ONLY-QUEEN GUARD: after every trade re-count guards; a piece whose only guard is my queen is lost when his second attacker lands (13.g5?? Bxd4).
- MATE FIRST: after every his move ask what mates and what a pawn push OPENS (23...g6; exf6 -> Qxg7#; Rd7 -> Rd8#).
- DOWN MATERIAL: trade pieces, no grabs, blockade - vs SF/Sonnet expect NO repetition rescue. R-vs-B endings: bishop OFF the rook's open lines; KING blockades passers.
