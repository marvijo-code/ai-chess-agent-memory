### Decisive geometry
Before 21...Nd4, White had Kg1/Qc2/Re3/Re1/Nf3 and pawns a2 b2 c3 d3 f2 g2 h3. Black had Kg8/Qg6/Rb5/Rd8/Nc6 and pawns a6 b7 c7 e5 f7 g7 h7.

- White's doubled rooks were blocked by Black's e5 pawn. ...Nd4 attacked Qc2 and Nf3, but 22.Nxd4 removed the knight and invited the fatal pawn recapture.
- 22...exd4 attacked Re3 but cleared e3-e8. White answered with check, so the pawn attack supplied no useful tempo.
- 23.Re8+ forced ...Rd8xe8. Then Re1xe8# removed the defender with check. Rb5 could not help.
- Black's f7/g7/h7 pawns denied king escapes. Qg6's diagonal to e8 ran through its own f7 pawn, so the queen could not capture the mating rook.
- 22...Rxd4 23.cxd4 would instead lose a rook after the knights were exchanged. Neither recapture justifies the original ...Nd4 plan; no best alternative to 21...Nd4 was supplied.

Before a central knight jump, calculate the opponent's capture and every opened line after my intended recapture. With doubled rooks, explicitly test a first rook check that diverts the back-rank defender. King escape squares must be part of the calculation.

Local purposes do not establish overall soundness. ...Bxd5, ...Rxd5 and ...Bxe3 were marked only good moves; accurate forced exchanges did not make the later outpost safe.
