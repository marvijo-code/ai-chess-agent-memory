# Berlin setup: exchanges and lost defenders

## Sonnet 5.5, Armageddon as Black, checkmate loss
Black had draw odds and started with 7:30 against White's 10:00.

1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 Nf6 4.d3 Bc5 5.c3 O-O 6.O-O d6 7.Nbd2 a6 8.Ba4 b5 9.Bc2 Bb7 10.Re1 Re8 11.Nf1 d5 12.Ng3 dxe4 13.dxe4 Qxd1 14.Rxd1 Rad8 15.Be3 Bxe3 16.fxe3 Rxd1+ 17.Rxd1.

## Decisive exchange error
17...Rd8?! 18.Rxd8+ Nxd8! 19.Nxe5 Nxe4?? 20.Nxe4 Bxe4 21.Bxe4.

- Nc6 defended e5. The forced recapture on d8 removed that defense, allowing Nf3xe5. Assess the resulting position before offering the rook exchange.
- At e4, White had Ng3 and Bc2 against Black's Nf6 supported by Bb7. Nd8 did not attack e4.
- After Ng3xe4, ...Bb7xe4 and Bc2xe4, Black had traded a knight and bishop for one knight. White retained Ne5 and Be4 against Nd8: an extra bishop, with equal pawn counts.
- The thought that bishop support kept the capture sound omitted White's final recapture. Tally the whole sequence, not just the first exchange.
- 18...Nxd8 received the only-good-move mark. Prevent the problem earlier; do not blame or avoid a forced recapture. No alternative to 17...Rd8 is established here.

21...Ne6 22.Bd5 Kf8 23.Bxe6 fxe6 left White a knight against pawns. White exchanged the remaining Black piece and centralized its king.

## Queenless mating net
With White Kf4/Nf3 and pawns a2 b2 c3 e3 g3 h4, Black Kd5 and pawns a5 b5 c5 e6 g6 h5:
31.e4+ Kc4 32.Ne5#.

Escape map from c4: b3 attacked by a2; b4/d4 by c3; d5 by e4; d3 by Ne5; c3 defended by b2; b5/c5 occupied by Black pawns. Calculate enemy knight checks before entering a pawn enclosure.

Black still had over five minutes at mate. The decisive failure was exchange calculation, not time pressure. Sonnet made no marked errors in this game; do not expect rescue after losing material.
