### Queen check captured by the distant rook
- Qe7 defended Bb4 along e7-d6-c5-b4 and e6 down the e-file. Rad8 supported central play, but White still took b7/a7; no sound compensation was established.
- f4 blocked Bd6's mating diagonal. This time I avoided the earlier ...Bxf4 bishop exchange, but the subsequent attack still failed.
- ...e5 attacked Qd4 and f4, yet was marked a mistake. White answered with Qc4+, gaining a check. No best replacement or precise reason for the mark was supplied.
- Before ...Qe1+, White had Kg1/Qc4/Ra1/Rf3/Be3. Squares b1, c1 and d1 were empty. Ra1 could therefore capture the queen on e1 immediately.
- Rf3 had vacated f1; focusing on that rook and a supposed forced interposition overlooked Ra1. A checking square must be tested against captures by every enemy piece.
- 22...fxe3 took only Be3, not a rook. After Rxf8+ Bxf8, White retained Q+R against R+B, plus an extra pawn. The move mark was only ?, but the queen loss is explicit.
- ...Qe1+ took 50 seconds; Black still had 11:59 afterward. This was a capture-scan failure with ample time.

### Resistance and result
24.b4 Re8 25.Qd4 h6 26.Kf1 Bd6 27.Ke2 Bg3 28.Rd1 Bf2 created mutually supporting Bf2/e3. ...Rb8 challenged b5; the king advanced through h7-g7-g6-h5-g4. White then alternated queen checks while Black shuttled between h5/g4; the recorded result was threefold repetition.

The depth-4 ladder's displayed evaluations remained roughly +9 to +11 for White after the queen loss. This was practical survival in a lost position, not compensation or a theoretically forced draw. Keep creating obstacles when behind, but reject the queen blunder in future games. Finished with 9:50 and no illegal attempts.

White repeated; the result does not establish a theoretical draw. Finished with 6:45.
