### Bishop retreat blocked by my rook
After 27.Rg1:
- White: Kd3, Rf5/Rg1, Bd4; pawns a2 b2 c2 d5 h4.
- Black: Kd7, Re8/Rg8, Bh6; pawns a7 b7 c7 d6 f7 h5.

27...Rgf8?? 28.Rf6! Bf4 29.Rxf4 lost the bishop.
- Rf6 attacked Bh6 across the sixth rank. ...Rgf8 had occupied the bishop's f8 retreat.
- g7 was attacked by Rg1; g5 by Rg1 and h4; f4 by Rf6; e3 by Bd4; d2 by Kd3; c1 by Rg1 along the clear first rank.
- ...Bf4 moved directly onto an attacked square. After Rxf4, f7 blocked Rf8's line to f4, so the apparent rook alignment supplied no recapture.
- Do not activate a rook onto a friendly bishop's escape square without calculating the opponent's forcing attack. Reject this trap; no best replacement was supplied.
- ...Rgf8 took 52 seconds and left 9:52. The failure was concrete safety calculation, with ample time.

### Later forcing tactics
29...c5 30.dxc6+ Kxc6: dxc6 was en passant, removing c5 and checking Kd7 with the pawn on c6. Include en passant when testing double pawn pushes beside enemy pawns.

44.Bc5+ Kd7 45.Bxf8: Bc5 attacked Ke7 along c5-d6-e7, with Rf8 behind the king on the same diagonal. The king move exposed the rook to this bishop skewer.

49.Rxf4+ Kg7 50.Rg2+ Kh8 51.Rh4#: Rg2 controlled g7/g8; Rh4 checked along the h-file and covered h7. My h2 pawn blocked Rh1 from capturing or interposing above h4. Before chasing promotion, inspect rook switches that seal the king's neighboring file. Finished with 3:57 and no illegal attempts.

24...Rae8 was not check: Be7 blocked the e-file. 25...Bxg5+ vacated e7 and uncovered Re8's check on Ke2. Verify check geometry instead of trusting the move explanation.
