## Central play and forcing discovery
- Ng3 defended e4 but was marked a mistake; exd5 was inaccurate. Their local purposes did not establish overall soundness. No best replacements were supplied.
- Black's ...Nxd5 allowed Nc3xd5. Black then declined an immediate queen recapture and moved Be7 to f8.
- 21.Nf4 moved Nd5 onto a square attacked by Ne6. My claim that Bf2 could recapture was false: its relevant diagonals run through e3-d4-c5 and g3-h4, never f4.
- Bd3 blocked Rd1's line to Qd7. After ...Nxf4, Bh7+ removed that blocker with check; ...Kh8 Rxd7 Bxd7 traded my rook for Black's queen. This forcing resource does not validate the incorrect defender count on f4.

## Decisive pin error
After 38...Rg7:
- White: Kg1, Qb7, Ng3; pawns a2 b2 g2 h2.
- Black: Kh7, Rg7, Bf8; pawns a7 h6.
- The queen attacked Kh7 along b7-c7-d7-e7-f7-g7-h7, with Rg7 interposed.
- Rg7 was pinned along the seventh rank, but could move along that rank. ...Rxb7 could capture the pinning queen legally. After Qxa7, ...Rxa7 did exactly that; no White piece could recapture.
- A pin forbids exposing the king, not all movement. Capturing the attacker removes its attack entirely. Reject Qxa7 and find a safe queen response to the rook attack.
- The earlier Qe4/Rg6/Kh7 diagonal pin had to be discarded after the queen moved to b7 and the rook moved to g7. Never carry a remembered pin onto a changed board.
- After 37...Bxf8, White had Q+N against R+B, with pawns still present. The queen loss destroyed the remaining material advantage; collecting a7 was unnecessary without verifying safety.
- Qxa7 took twelve seconds with eight minutes remaining. This was a concrete pin-rule failure, not time pressure.

## Later knight loss
51...Rf2 pinned Nf4 to Kf5. After 52.g5 hxg5 53.Kxg5, the king move released the pin, but 53...Bd2 added a second attacker to Nf4.

- Nf4 was defended only by Kg5 and attacked by Rf2 and Bd2.
- 54.h5 ignored that imbalance. ...Bxf4+ won the knight, and Rf2 protected Bf4 along f2-f3-f4, so Kxf4 was illegal.
- Releasing a pin does not remove the attacks on the formerly pinned piece. Calculate the capture and recapture before advancing a passer.

Finished with 6:21 and no illegal attempts. Sonnet's opening mistakes allowed a forcing queen win, but it later coordinated rook and bishop and captured exposed material. Its resistance requires fresh safety checks at every move.
