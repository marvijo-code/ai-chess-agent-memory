## T7 round 1: White vs Sonnet, checkmate win
14.Nf1 Bd7 15.Ng3 Rac8 16.Be3?! Nxd4! 17.Nxd4? exd4 18.Qxd4 Qxc2 19.Rac1 Qc4?? 20.Rxc4! Rxc4 21.Qd3.
- Central liquidation removed the c-file blockers. Qxd4 recovered a pawn but abandoned Qd1's defense of Bc2. White lost a bishop; Rac1 did not establish a forced favorable queen exchange.
- Qc4 was defended by b5 and Rc8, yet Rxc4 won queen for rook. Do not automatically capture an offered queen with my queen. Qxc4 bxc4 would merely exchange queens. After Rxc4 Rxc4, Qxc4 still lost to bxc4; Qd3 preserved the queen.
- White then had Q+R+B+N against 2R+2B+N, with equal pawn counts. The queen gain was not a clean extra queen.

22.Nf5 Bxf5 23.exf5! h6 24.Bd4 Nd7?! 25.Rxe7 Nc5 26.Qg3 Rxd4 27.f6 g6 28.Qe3 Rh4 29.Re8+ Kh7 30.Rxc8.
- Nf5 threatened Nxe7+, forking king and Rc8. Bxf5 exf5 exchanged knight for bishop; it did not win material by itself.
- Bd4 cleared the e-file for Re1 against Be7. Nd7 neither blocked that file nor defended e7, so Rxe7 captured the bishop.
- Qg3 threatened Qxg7# with Bd4's diagonal support through e5/f6. But it left Bd4 undefended: Rxd4 won the bishop and removed that mate threat. This unmarked move still missed a concrete defensive capture.
- f6 renewed support of g7; ...g6 blocked the queen's g-file route. Qe3 attacked Rd4 and defended Re7. Re8+ Kh7 Rxc8 then collected the other rook.

30...Ne6 31.Qg3 Rd4 32.Re8 Nc7 33.Re7 Nd5 34.Rxf7+ Kh8 35.Qxg6 Rg4 36.Qh7#.
- f6 protected Re7 on e7 and supported a queen on g7. After Rxf7+ and Qxg6, both Qg7# and Qh7# were threatened.
- Rg4 attacked Qg6 and covered g7 once the queen moved, but did not stop Qh7#. Rf7 protected Qh7 through empty g7; Qh7 also controlled g8, while g7 was covered by rook and pawn.
- Finished with 9:03 and no illegal attempts. Familiar opening moves took 3-8 seconds. Several exchanges and attacking moves took about 53 seconds; extra thought did not prevent Nxd4 or Qg3 errors.

## T6 semifinal: White vs Sonnet, loss
14.Nb3 a5 15.Be3 a4 16.Nbd2 Bb7 17.d5 Nb4 18.Bb1! Rfc8 19.a3 Nc2 20.Bxc2 Qxc2! 21.Qxc2 Rxc2!.
- Saving Bc2 did not prevent Nc2's attacks on both rooks and Be3. Liquidation left Black active against b2/Nd2.
- Later Nxc5 dxc5 cleared Bc8-d7-e6-f5; Nf5 attacked Be7 but allowed the OTHER bishop's Bxf5. exf5 removed e4's support of d5.
- The exchange created White d5 and Black c5 passers. Evaluate blockades, king access, and promotion routes before racing.
- With Black c3/Ba5 and White Kc4, Bd2?? allowed cxd2. Kc4 could not recapture d2; removing c3 opened Ba5-b4-c3-d2 and protected the passer. Lost with ample time.

## Earlier recurring errors
- Black Qb6 behind White d4 on Be3's diagonal allowed dxe5, attacking queen and Nf6 together; Qd8 saved queen but lost knight.
- ...Nxe3 Qxe3 Nxe5 Nxe5 Bc5 Qxc5 Qxc5 Nxc5 Rxc5 lost a knight. Be7 blocked Re8's apparent e5 defense.
- Rb3 allowed direct Rxb3; ...Rc1+ missed it. ...Rb1 later allowed Rb3xb1 through empty b2. Compare captures before checks.
- Bd3 cleared Rc1's last blocker against Qc7; ...Nxb4 ignored Rxc7. Qa4 lost to bxa4; Nb3 overlooked axb3.
- Nh4-f5 Bxf5 Ng3xf5, f3-f4, and Re3-g3 remove e4 defenders. Recount before attacking.
- Game 4 won material through discovered checks but flagged with queen against bare king. Execute verified elementary mates promptly.
