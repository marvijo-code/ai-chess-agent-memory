# Four Knights as Black - 4.Bb5 Bb4 (game 3, lost 0-1)
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bb5 Bb4 5.O-O O-O 6.Nd5 Nxd5 7.exd5 Ne7 8.Nxe5 Nxd5 9.Bc4 Nf6 10.c3 Bc5 11.d4 Bb6 12.Bd3 d6 13.Nc4!

## The b6-bishop trap (the game-losing moment)
- 13.Nc4 hits b6; Bb6 has no free square: a5 (knight), c5 (d4-pawn), d4/e3 (cxd4/Nxe3), a7/c7 own pawns, d8 own queen and the c7-pawn blocks b6-d8.
- Correct: 13...b5! (also fine one move later, after 13...Bg4 14.Qc2) attacks the c4-knight. 14.Nxb6 axb6/cxb6 = bishop for knight, equal. If the knight retreats, the bishop lives.
- Do NOT: 13...Bg4 then 14...Bxd4?? - the c3-pawn recaptures: bishop for a pawn, eval jumped +0.97 -> +7.9 and the game was over. No cash-in captures on pawn-defended squares.

## Earlier flow (was OK)
- 6...Nxd5 7...Ne7 8...Nxd5 wins the d5-pawn back; 9...Nf6 10...Bc5 11...Bb6 12...d6 is solid, White only ~+0.5.
- Develop the c8-bishop (...Bg4 pin) only once Bb6 is safe.

## After the blunder (do not repeat)
- 15...Ne4 hung the knight (16.Bxe4, nothing could recapture): check every destination square for attackers with no defender.
- 19...Qxe4?? Rxe4: the Re3 guarded e4. List defenders of the capture square, not just the target's value.
- Down material: play fast and solid; game 3 burned ~4 min thinking in a dead-lost position.
