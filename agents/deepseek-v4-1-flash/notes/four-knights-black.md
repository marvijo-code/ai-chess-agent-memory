# Four Knights as Black - 4.Bb5 Bb4 (g3, g4 both lost 0-1)
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bb5 Bb4 5.O-O O-O 6.Nd5 Nxd5 7.exd5 Ne7

## Move 8 - 8.Nxe5 (both games): stay calm
- 8.Nxe5 wins the e5 pawn (the c6-knight traded itself on d5; the e7-knight doesn't defend e5). Eval only ~+0.5 - playable.
- Answer: 8...Nxd5 (both games; regains the d5-pawn) or ...d6/...c6 hitting the e5-knight. g3 flow 8...Nxd5 9.Bc4 Nf6 10.c3 Bc5 11.d4 Bb6 12.Bd3 d6 was solid (~+0.5).
- NEVER grab a pawn with a piece as compensation: g4 8...Bxd2?? 9.Qxd2 was bishop for a pawn (+0.5 -> +6.9) and decided the game. d2/c2/f2 are guarded by the queen in the opening.

## The b6-bishop trap (g3)
- 13.Nc4! hits Bb6, which has no free square: a5/c5/d4/e3 covered, a7/c7 own pawns, c7-pawn blocks the d8 exit.
- Correct: 13...b5! (also fine one move later after 13...Bg4 14.Qc2) attacks the c4-knight; 14.Nxb6 axb6/cxb6 = bishop for knight, equal.
- Do NOT play 13...Bg4 then 14...Bxd4?? cxd4 (bishop for pawn; +1 -> +7.9). No cash-in captures on pawn-defended squares.

## After blunders (do not repeat)
- 15...Ne4?? Bxe4: check every destination square for attackers with no defender.
- 19...Qxe4?? Rxe4: list defenders of the capture square, not just the target's value.
- g4 extras: 10...Nf4?? Qxf4; 12...Qxe5?? Qxe5 (queen for knight); 13...Re8?? Qxe8# (rook to a queen-attacked square, undefended, back-rank mate).
- Down material: play fast, defend loose pieces, no tilt captures.
