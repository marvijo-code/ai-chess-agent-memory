# English reversed QGD as Black v GLM 5.3 Flash (T50R3, 0-1 checkmate m56)

Opening played, 15 plies: 1.c4 Nf6 2.Nc3 e6 3.g3 d5 4.cxd5 exd5 5.Nf3 Be7 6.Bg2 O-O 7.O-O c6 8.d3. Then ...Bf5 9.d4 Nbd7 10.Bf4 Re8 11.Re1 Qb6 - a sound reversed-QGD setup, level to move 11.

## What won material
- 12...Qxd4! : his d4 pawn had no defender because HIS Nd2 blocked Qd1's line. Count his guards with screens; a blocked guard is no guard.
- 15.Nxd5! won the pawn back when my earlier ...c5 had taken the c6 guard off d5. ...c5 needs ...c6 or ...Ne4 support first.
- 18...Nxd5! : his Bd5 was guarded only by his queen, and my Be6 covered d5, so Qxd5?? Bxd5 lost his queen. This 'queen-only defender + my capturer's square covered' pattern won a second bishop. Knight geometry: f6 hits d5/e4/d7/e8/g4/g8/h5/h7.
- ...Nxf4, ...Bxh4 (g3 vacated), ...Bxb3 then queens off left R+B+2P v R+R: a won ending.

## The throw
Up a piece since move 18, R+B+4P v R+2P at move 44. I played 44...Ra4+?? - a CHECK chosen only to drive his king off c4 - and 45.bxa4 took a whole rook: a4 is the capture square of his b3 pawn. Then his a-pawn ran: a5/a6/a7/a8=Q while my ...f5/...f4/...f3 race arrived too late; ...Bf8/...Bd6 hung, and Q+R mated with Rd8#.
Rules for this structure: (1) every rook/queen landing square gets the enemy PAWN capture-square sweep first, checks included; (2) when up material with his passer on the 6th, the rook or bishop holds a6/a7 and my king comes - do not race pawns; (3) never write 'the rook is safe' without naming what attacks its square.

## Opponent note (GLM 5.3 Flash)
