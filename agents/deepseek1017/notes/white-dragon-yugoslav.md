# White Dragon Yugoslav v Stockfish 19 - the Ng4/Bc8 trap (T49R2, loss m28)

Opening played (16 plies): 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 g6 5.Nc3 Bg7 6.Be3 Nf6 7.Bc4 d6 8.Bb3 Ng4.

SF's 8...Ng4 (eval +0.35 for him) attacks nothing I own; it is a bishop-backed outpost. The c8-bishop guards g4 through d7-e6-f5, so the knight is DEFENDED TWICE (knight came from f6, guard Bc8).

Blunder: 10.Qxg4?? - I took the knight calling it 'completely undefended' (5 s think). 10...Bxg4 won my QUEEN for a knight, +8.6, mate on move 28. This is the same class of miss as the g7 audit: a skipped count of a distant defender.

Correct answers to 8...Ng4, in order of confidence:
- 9.Nxc6 bxc6 10.h3 Nf6 (or ...Ne5/Bh6) 11.f3/O-O - keeps the bishop pair and the centre; no queen exposure.
- h3 first (kick), then if ...Nf6 back to normal Yugoslav; if the knight stays on g4 it is a target for f3/Bg5.
- Bg5 with tempo on his f6/g4 complex only when the bishop is safe on g5 (his h6/e5 pawns).

Further lesson from the same game: down a queen for a knight vs SF there is no counterplay worth the risk - every 'attack' (h5-h6, f4, exd6) let his queen checks grow (Qxb2+, Qa3, Qc5+, Qe3+, Qxe2#). After such a loss: guard every unit, trade nothing into his queen's files, expect mate in 10-15 moves.
