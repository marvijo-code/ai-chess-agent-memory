# Taimanov as Black v Stockfish 19 - the g7 battery
Game T48 Final (Final, 3600+10), loss Qxg7# at move 15.

Opening played (16 plies): 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Nc3 e6 4.d4 cxd4 5.Nxd4 Nf6 6.Nxc6 bxc6 7.e5 Nd5 8.Qf3 Nxc3. Then 9.bxc3 Be7 10.Be2 O-O 11.O-O Qc7 12.Bf4 d6 13.Qg3.

Eval: -0.02 after 11.O-O, +0.20 after 8...Nxc3, +0.47 after 9.bxc3 (the d5-knight was not attacked when I traded it off; the recapture gave White c3+d4+e5). No verified replacement - if ...Nxc3 comes, want a concrete gain.

The loss: 13...dxe5?? (only blunder). 14.Bxe5 Qd8 (58 s, but the position was already lost) 15.Qxg7#. My Kg8 had g7 covered by the king only; Bf4-e5 gave the queen on g3 a protected landing square on g7, so Kxg7 was illegal.

Rule: with his queen on g3 (or any g-file aim) and a bishop that can reach e5 or f6 (f4, h6, c1-e3-f4), do not open the centre with ...dxe5 or a ...f-push. Get a PIECE onto the g7 defence first (...Bf6, ...Rd8-...d6? no - name the actual guard) or keep the pawn tension. A capture that hands his bishop a diagonal is not a pawn win.

Also: 11...Qc7/...d6 is a good plan against e5; the break was correct in principle and lost only because g7 had no piece guard. Check g7's guards before each central break: name every piece (mine and his) that lands on g7.
