# White Taimanov v GLM 5.3 Flash (T49R4, win on time)

Opening played, 16 plies: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 a6 6.Be2 Qc7 7.O-O Nf6 8.Be3 Be7. Then 9.Qd2 O-O 10.f4 d6 11.Rad1 b5 - the Taimanov setup with ...Qc7 early instead of ...Be7/...O-O first; it holds and is playable for Black.

## The pattern that won material: in-between check
11...b5 12.Nxc6 Nxe4. My c6-knight was undefended and his e4-knight hit my queen, so I did NOT resolve the queen attack first - 13.Nxe7+! took the bishop with check. 13...Qxe7 was forced (8:07 think) and 14.Nxe4 picked up the e4-knight: two pieces for a knight, ~+5 by move 14. Lesson confirmed: when his capture attacks my queen, look for a CHECK that grabs material before recapturing; that theme beat GLM here, and theory says 'a knight attacking my queen is capture-first' only if no bigger in-between exists.

## The one real miss: 21.Qd3?? cxd3
Material was +R+B for me and I centralized the queen onto d3. His c4-pawn (from 20...bxc4) attacked d3; only my c2-pawn 'guarded' it, so 21...cxd3 won my QUEEN for a pawn. Same class as T49R2 Qxg4?? and T49R3 Qxd8+: before ANY queen move list every enemy PAWN's forward-diagonal squares from the board, then sliders, and only then decide. Rule: a queen square attacked by a pawn is a capture square, never a 'centralizing' square - and a pawn 'defender' does not make it safe, the pawn is simply taken.

## Recovery (queen for a rook+pawn, held to his flag)
- After the queen went I kept every unit guarded, 5-40 s moves, and let his clock burn: he spent 4:44, 8:07, 5:21, 7:02 on quiet moves and flagged with 0:00 while I had ~49 min.
- His ...d4 push entombed my Bc3 on d2 (28.Bd2 was the only square); I then won the loose Bc4 with 29.Rxc4 and later the b2-rook with 33.Bc1/34.Bxb2, reaching 2R+N v Q+pawns.
- Useful defensive geometry v a queen: rooks on c7/e1, knight g3-e4 hitting f6, bishop d2 blocking the passed d-pawn, king h1. His Qb6+/Qd5/Qd6 shuffles never cracked it.
