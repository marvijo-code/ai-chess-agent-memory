## T22 semifinal 2 game 1: White vs Sonnet, mate win
4.d3 d6 5.O-O Bd7 6.Re1 Be7 7.c3 O-O 8.d4 Re8 9.Nbd2 Bf8 10.Bd3 h6 11.Nf1 exd4 12.cxd4 Nb4 13.Bb1 d5 14.e5! Ne4 15.Ng3 Nxg3 16.hxg3 c5 17.Be3 cxd4 18.Bxd4 Nc6 19.Qd3 g6! 20.Bc3 Bg7 21.Qxd5 Be6 22.Qxd8 Raxd8.
- e5 gained tempo and cleared Bb1-h7. Qd3 threatened Qh7#, forcing a defensive reply before saving Bd4; ...g6 blocked the battery. Sonnet answered this threat correctly: do not expect the earlier Chigorin mating error to recur.
- Qxd5 won a pawn; queen exchange reduced counterplay. This supports a playable setup, not a forced opening advantage. hxg3 did not immediately provide a rook attack on the h-file: the rook was on f1.
23.Bc2 Bd5 24.Rad1 Bxf3 25.Rxd8 Rxd8! 26.gxf3! Rd5? 27.f4 Nd4 28.Be4! Rd7 29.Bxd4?! Rxd4! 30.Bxb7! Rb4 31.Bd5 Rxb2 32.Rd1 Rxa2?! 33.Bxa2.
- The intermediate rook exchange kept Re1 defending e5 while recovering Bf3. Recalculate the capture order rather than automatically recapturing first.
- f4 reinforced e5. Be4 saved Bc2 from Nd4 and attacked Rd5 with tempo. Look for a safe retreat that also improves activity.
- Bxd4 was marked an inaccuracy despite the win. Bxb7 was the only good reply after ...Rxd4, but ...Rb4 attacked Bb7/b2 and regained a pawn. Do not justify the knight exchange solely by the b7 capture; no verified better move supplied.
- Bd5 guarded a2 through c4/b3. Sonnet spent 55 seconds on ...Rxa2 but missed Bxa2, losing its rook. Recheck every bishop ray before pawn grabs; the win depended on this later error.
34.Rd7 f5 35.Rxa7 h5 36.e6 Bd4 37.Rf7+ Kg8 38.e7 Kh8 39.e8=Q#.
- e6 supported Rf7's checking retreat from Bd4's attack on Ra7. Advancing e7 removed that pawn guard but opened Ba2-b3-c4-d5-e6-f7: the bishop replaced it, so Kxf7 remained illegal.
- At promotion Qe8 covered g8/h8; Rf7 covered g7/h7. Name the surviving guards after each pawn advance. No invalid attempts; about 13 minutes remained. Rad1 took 52 seconds, but routine conversion mostly took 3-13.

## T21 semifinal 2 game 1: White vs Sonnet, mate loss
...Bd7 d4 exd4 cxd4 d5 e5 Ne4 Nc3 Nxc3 bxc3! Nxe5! Rxe5 Bxb5! Rxd5?? Qxd5.
- bxc3 was the only good move. Nxe5 vacated c6, opening Bd7-c6-b5 while taking e5. Rxe5 Bxb5 exchanged N for B, leaving material level.
- OWN d4 blocked Qd1-d5: Rxd5 attacked Q but had no queen recapture. Assess a safe rook retreat; Re1 is legal, not engine-verified best.
- Qc2 Rfe8 Bf4 Bd6 Ng5 g6 Ne4 Qxe4 Qxe4 Rxe4 lost N. Re8 backed Qd5's capture through e7/e6/e5; the fork threat did not prevent capture.
- Ke3/Rf2 allowed ...Bc5+ along c5-d4-e3-f2; Ke2 Bxf2 Kxf2 gave R for B. A king screen disappears when check forces it away.
- Finished 15:17: imagined recapture paths and missed exchanges, not clock shortage.
