## T24 final: Black vs Stockfish 19, mate loss
4.O-O Nxe4 5.d4 Nd6 6.Bxc6 dxc6 7.dxe5 Nf5 8.Qe2 Be7?! 9.Rd1! Nd4?? 10.Nxd4 Qd5.
- With queens retained, d7/d6/d5/d4/d3/d2 were empty before Nd4. Rd1 directly attacked Qd8. Routine bishop development had allowed a forcing rook tempo; castling intentions did not solve the current queen attack.
- Nd4 blocked the file and attacked Qe2, but Nf3 could capture it. The apparent c6 pawn defender was relatively pinned to Qd8: ...cxd4 vacates the d-file screen and allows Rxd8+ Bxd8, losing Q for R. Calculate the recapture's vacated line, not just its landing square. Black instead abandoned the knight with Qd5.
- Be7 was marked inaccurate, Rd1 the only good move, and Nd4 a blunder. No verified best Black replacement supplied. Do not treat this queens-on position as the normal queenless Berlin ending.
11.Rd2 O-O 12.c4 Qc5 13.Nc3 Bg5 14.Rd1 Rd8 15.Nb3 Qe7 16.Rxd8+ Qxd8 17.f4 Be7 18.Bd2 Be6 19.g3 f6 20.exf6 Bxf6 21.Qxe6+.
- The rook exchange did not restore the lost knight. Keep an explicit total material count rather than calling a locally equal exchange a recovery.
- Be6 relied on f7's pawn guard; White's e5 pawn also screened Qe2-e3-e4-e5-e6. ...f6 removed the guard AND enabled exf6 to remove the screen. Bxf6 did not protect Be6, and Qxe6+ won another bishop. Before a pawn break, examine the enemy's resulting capture and every exposed friendly piece.

- Bxc3 Bxc3 traded the remaining bishop for a knight; it did not recover the earlier deficit. Bc3 attacked Qb4 and was guarded by b2, so ...Qxc3 would lose Q to bxc3.
- Qh5 supported e8 through g6/f7. Re8+ forced Ra8 to capture, then Qxe8# removed the back-rank defender. ...Kg8 released g7's bishop pin but did not answer this mate threat. Trace queen diagonals and forced rook deflections before a king move or pawn grab.
- No invalid attempts; 13:50 remained. Nd4 took 17 seconds, Qd5 23, ...f6 26: ample time did not replace full recapture and threat calculation.
