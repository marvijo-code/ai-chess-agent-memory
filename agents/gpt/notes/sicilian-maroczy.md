# Open Sicilian: central pressure, remote rays and conversion

## T43 round 1: White vs GLM, Taimanov, time win
- Qc7/a6/Nf6/Be7 met Be2/O-O/Be3/f4/Kh1. Nxd4 Bxd4 kept the bishop active. Against Bc5?!, e5 attacked Nf6 while Bd4 was attacked; after Nd5, Nxd5 exd5! Bxc5 Qxc5! left Black doubled d-pawns. No White moves marked adverse; the result does not establish a forced opening advantage.
- Bf3 added a second attacker to d5: Qd1 and Bf3 versus Qc5 alone. b5?! prepared Bb7 but supplied no immediate defense. Qxd5 won the pawn; Qxd5 Bxd5 would also attack Ra8. A queen's sole defense does not save a pawn from a queen capture backed by a minor piece.
- b7-b5 cleared d5-c6-b7-a8. Qxc2? regained a pawn but abandoned the remote rook: Qxa8 won it. Trace a centralized queen's full diagonal after an enemy pawn push, even when the first target was a pawn.
- Qxb2/Rab1/Qxa2 left me R for two pawns ahead. Qd5 offered a supported queen trade; Qxd5 Bxd5 retained the surplus and active bishop. Do not keep queens merely to defend every pawn when a safe exchange removes counterplay.
- Rfc1 contested the open file. d6 exd6 created my passer; Bd7 blockaded it. Rc7 attacked the blockader, forcing Bc8. Rbc1 doubled rooks; after Rd8, Rxc8 Rxc8 Rxc8+ removed B and Black's last rook. My remaining rook supported d8=Q. Finish the whole exchange chain: exchanging one surplus rook can remove both the blockade and enemy heavy piece.
- Won on time after Qe8+, with Q+R+B versus pawns; no mate was played. Zero invalid; 57:23 remained in 3600+10. GLM repeatedly spent 3-9 minutes and had one invalid attempt. My verified late exchanges/promotion took 3-11 seconds; several routine moves still used 23-39. Keep the scan concise even with an hour available.

## T42 round 3: White vs GLM, Scheveningen, mate win
- cxd4/Nxd4 occurred, unlike T41. Be2/O-O/Be3/f4 met Nxd4; Bxd4 kept B active. After e5 attacked B, fxe5 dxe5! Bxe5 left me P ahead. No White adverse marks; not proof of forced advantage.
- Nd7 attacked Be5; Bg3 opened g3-f4-e5-d6-c7-b8. Qc7?? landed on that ray: Bxc7 won Q. Bd6 later landed on clear Qd1-d6: Qxd6 won B.
- b6 did not attack Bc7: Black's b6-pawn captures a5/c5. Derive pawn attacks independently of comments.
- Qg3/Bc4/Rxf7 built Qxg7#. Bc4 supported Rf7 through d5/e6; Rf7 supported g7 horizontally. Nf6 saved Nd7 but did not stop mate. Distinguish this from the Bd4/Qg3 battery that Nf6 can block.

## T42 round 2: White Maroczy vs SF19, mate loss
- c4/Nc3 met Qa5. f3 guarded e4 because Nc3 was pinned to Ke1; after Nxd4, Qxd4! was the only good move. Qd2?! inaccurate; no verified replacement.
- Be2/O-O met Be6/Bxc4. Bxc4 Qc5+ checked Kg1 through d4/e3/f2 AND attacked Bc4; f3 had vacated f2. Anticipating a check is insufficient without its continuation.
- Kh1 Qxc4 exposed Rf1 on c4-d3-e2-f1. Be2 had vacated e2, Kh1 abandoned Rf1, and Bc1 blocked Ra1-f1. b3 chased Q but allowed Qxf1#. Reconstruct the queen's NEW rays after a checking pickup. Loss does not refute 5.c4.
