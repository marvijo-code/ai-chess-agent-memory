# Open Sicilian: screens, central captures and promotion

## T40 round 4: White vs GLM, mate win
- Against Nc6/Nf6/e6, Ndb5 induced d6; Be2/O-O/Be3 followed. After a6, Nd4! Nxd4 Bxd4 e5 Be3! kept the bishop. This differs from my usual quiet Be2-first Taimanov. f4/exf4/Bxf4 challenged the center.
- Qb6+ required Kh1!; Qxb2 attacked Nc3. Qd3?! defended it but was inaccurate; no verified replacement supplied. Qb6?! then allowed Nd5, attacking Qb6/Be7/Nf6. Nxd5 exd5! attacked Be6. Rfd8?? ignored that attack: dxe6 won B, and d6 blocked Rd8's path to Qd3. Rook activity did not save the minor.
- Bf6 aimed through e5/d4/c3/b2 at Ra1. Rab1 escaped with tempo against Qb6. It did NOT answer an attack on Bf4: that bishop was guarded by Rf1 through empty f2/f3. Reconstruct actual rays instead of accepting opponent commentary.
- Qd4 offered a safe queen exchange with Bf6 supporting it. Qxd4/Bxd4 reduced counterplay while B ahead. exf7+ preserved the advanced pawn; Rxb7 recovered another pawn. Rd7 was undefended: Ra8 was off its rank and Kf8 did not guard d7, so Rxd7 won a whole rook.
- Bxe5/dxe5 removed Black's bishop and vacated f4 for Rf1-f5. Rd7 guarded f7 through e7. After Rxe5, Re8 offered a rook that the passer could replace: Raxe8 fxe8=Q+ removed Black's last rook and promoted. Leaving f7 uncovered Rd7's check against Kg7. Scan capture-promotions AND their vacated rays.
- Qf8+/Rd5+/Qf4# drove K to h4. Rd5 controlled rank 5; Qf4 covered g4/g3/h4/h6; g2 controlled h3. Name flights and captures before declaring mate.
- Zero invalid moves; about 11:50 remained. Qd3 took 61 seconds; Rf5/Re8 took 36/37 despite overwhelming material. The inaccurate defense needed a concrete comparison; checked conversion needed faster execution.

## T39 Maroczy vs SF19
- Qa5 pinned Nc3; f3 guarded e4, and Qxd4 was required after Nxd4. Short castling avoided earlier exposure.
- After b3/Ng4/Bd4/Ne5-c6, Bf2 opened Qa5-b4-c3 and lost Nc3. Qd1 did not guard c3. Rc1 hit Qc3 but Bh6 reached c1 through g5/f4/e3/d2; Bxc1 Qxc1 cost the exchange.
- Qh3 landed on Be6-f5-g4-h3 and lost Q to Bxh3. f4 did NOT block f5. Later Bc4 tried to stop a3-a2 but stood loose on Rf4's rank; Rxc4 won B. Purpose does not make a destination safe.

## Retained patterns
- Quiet Be2/O-O/Be3/f4/Kh1/Bf3 gives central pressure. Qe1 was inaccurate in T36/T38; after Qxe5 compare immediate queen exchange rather than automatic Nxc6.
- e5 hits Nf6 and clears Bf3's diagonal. With f6 clear, Qg3/Bxe5 can open mate; Nf6 blocks the battery. Qg3 against Kg8 can pin g7; Kf8 releases that pin.
- Qd5 hit Qe6/Rd7 with Nc3/Rd1 support, yet Rxd5 won Q for R because Black could stop exchanging. Re7 also landed on Nd5's capture square.
- Maroczy Ng4 hits Be3 and uncovers Bg7 against Qd4. Long castling: Nc3 screens Qa5-e1; Rd1 guards Re1. Nxd5 or Bd3/Bxd3/Rxd3 abandons those duties.
- Short castling: Nd5 clears Qa5-d2; queen exchange/Bxd5/exd5 can enable Bd4+ against Kg1/Ra1 when b2 is empty. c5 allows Rxc5; Bc4 loses to b5xc4. Guarding d1 does not stop capture-promotion there.
