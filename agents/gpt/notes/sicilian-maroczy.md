# Open Sicilian: screens, captures and blockades

## T39 round 2: White Maroczy vs SF19, mate loss
- c4/Nc3 met Qa5; f3 supplied e4's legal guard. Qxd4 was required after Nxd4. Qd2/Be2/O-O avoided the earlier long-castling exposure. The opening preserved material; the loss does not refute the bind.
- With b3 already played, Ng4 attacked Be3 and required Bd4. Bh6 then attacked Qd2. Qd3 and Qd1 were marked inaccurate; no verified replacements supplied. Do not store these retreats as book.
- DECISIVE SCREEN: Bd4 stood between Qa5 and Nc3 on a5-b4-c3. After Ne5-c6 attacked Bd4, Bf2 saved B but opened Qxc3. b3 had removed b2's pawn defense of c3. Qd1 did not guard c3. Scan the old square's rays and remote targets before choosing a retreat.
- Rc1 attacked Qc3 but Bh6 reached c1 through empty g5/f4/e3/d2. Bxc1 Qxc1 cost R for B, adding an exchange deficit to the lost knight. A queen attack does not force retreat when another piece captures the attacker.
- MAJOR UNMARKED MISS: Qh3 landed on Be6-f5-g4-h3. Bxh3 gxh3 lost Q for B. My f-pawn was on f4, not f5; it did not block this diagonal. Every queen destination needs every bishop's full rays, including a bishop remote from the king attack.
- Rxc4/bxc4 recovered one rook. Later gxh7+ discovered Rg1's check, but Qxg1+/Kxg1 left Black R versus my B. Taking a queen did not equalize the ending.
- Bd3 guarded h7 while Kh8 blockaded it. The light bishop could not control h8; my h-pawns had no independent promotion route. Black could use its rook and queenside passer while my bishop retained this duty.
- Bc4 intended to stop a3-a2 but landed on Rf4's clear fourth rank: f4-e4-d4-c4. Ke3 did not guard c4. Rxc4 removed B and the a-pawn promoted. A blockading purpose does not excuse an undefended destination; compare the rook's captures and the king's arrival before relocating B.
- Zero invalid moves; finished 7:31. Bd4 took 50 seconds, Qh3 37, Bc4 32. Time shortage did not cause the misses. Spend the scan on screens, bishop rays and recapturers, then shorten routine defense.

## T38/T37/T36 Taimanov lessons
- Be2/O-O/Be3/f4/Kh1/Bf3 gives playable central pressure. Qe1 was inaccurate in T36 and T38; compare central play instead of automatically routing Qe1-g3. After Qxe5, the T36 Nxc6 detour was inaccurate; compare immediate queen exchange.
- e5 hits Nf6 and clears Bf3's diagonal. Against d6, exf6 took N rather than the assumed exd6 and supported Qxg7#. Wins depended on Black's errors.
- With Qg3/Kg8 and a clear g-file, g7 is absolutely pinned. Nxf6+ can remove B and fork Re8 without legal gxf6. Kf8 releases the pin but may abandon Re8 to Nxe8. Recount actual pieces after king recaptures.
- Qd5 attacked Qe6/Rd7 and had Nc3/Rd1 support, but Rxd5 could win Q for R. Black could stop exchanging and keep Qe6. Cheapest enemy captures first; support does not make an unfavorable trade safe.
- Re7 landed on Nd5's capture square. Before moving a supporting knight, save the rook or name its replacement guard. Bxh6 can replace g7 support and free a knight for mate.

## Maroczy geometry to retain
- Qa5 BEFORE f3 pins Nc3 to Ke1; Be3 leaves e4 without a legal guard. With f3/Qxd4, Ng4 hits Be3 AND uncovers Bg7 against Qd4.
- O-O-O/Rhe1: Nc3 screens Qa5-e1; Rd1 guards Re1. Nxd5 removes the screen; Bd3/Bxd3/Rxd3 abandons Re1's last guard. Central exchanges can clear the c-file around Kc1.
- Rfd1 allowed Bxc4 Bxc4 Rxc4, losing P. Nd5 clears Rc8-c1; guarding d1 does not stop dxc1=Q.
- Short castling: Nd5 clears Qa5-d2; queen exchange and Bxd5/exd5 enable Bd4+, forking Kg1/Ra1 with b2 empty. c5 allows Rxc5; Bc4 loses to b5xc4. Re1 ignored Rf7-f4 against Bf4.
