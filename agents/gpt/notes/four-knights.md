- Later Qg4/Qxh4+ lost Q to g3xh4. The standing pawn's capture answered check directly. A queen beside K is not an attack when a pawn takes it; enumerate captures of the checker BEFORE king replies.
- White later gifted Qf8+ to Rxf8. This still left 2R versus R, not equality. Count the actual board after a windfall rather than assuming the earlier deficit vanished.
- Rg2 was supported by h3; Kg4-g3 and h2 created a promotion threat. White's Rc4+/Rb3+ drove K to f2, then Rc2+ drove it to rank 1 and Rb1# finished. Rc2 sealed rank 2; Rb1 checked along rank 1. My K supporting g1 and threatening Rg1 did not stop THEIR checks. The king needed an escape route before joining the pawn; no verified earlier repair supplied.
- Zero invalid attempts; finished 11:10. Book 3-6 seconds worked. Rf6 took 32 seconds; Qe5 took 53. Rb8/Qxh4+ took 15/20 and still omitted basic captures. Time remained ample: use the scan, not extra thought as reassurance.

## T36 final: lost ending, repetition
- After Bd6, d4 allowed Qh4 threatening Qxh2#; g3 allowed Qxd4 with tempo on Bc4. Bg4/Bxf3 and Bxd4 exchanged queens with an extra pawn. Matching board only.
- g6 supported f5 and screened Bc2-h7 but abandoned h6. Kingside exchanges opened the g-file; Re6/Rxh6+/Ree6 gave White two active sixth-rank rooks. Extra pawns did not make K safe.
- Kg7 screened my Rg8 from Kg1; Rg8 was not check. Kf7+ uncovered it. Bc6 saved B on a supported square but allowed Rhf6+ and king confinement.
- Rdf8 allowed Bh6+ Kh8 Bxf8 Bxf8 Rxc6: the bishop recapture vacated d6, exposing Bc6. Whole chain cost R+B for B. Audit both the checking ray and recapturer's departure.
- Supported a-passer forced Rxa1/Kxa1, but White's remaining h-pawn outran Ka1. Removing R did not save the pawn ending. Later Q versus bare K repetition was an escape from a theoretically lost position, not a fortress.
