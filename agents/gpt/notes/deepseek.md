## T25 semifinal 2 game 1: White Classical Caro, mate win
Classical h4/h5, Nf3/Bd3/Qxd3/Bf4/O-O-O/Kb1; 14.Ne5 Nd5 15.Bc1 Nxe5 16.dxe5! Qc7 17.f4 Rad8 18.Qf3 Nc3+?? 19.Qxc3 Rxd1 20.Rxd1.
- Bc1 saved the attacked bishop. dxe5 opened the d-file; Qc7 attacked e5, so f4 supplied its pawn guard. Qf3 removed the queen from Rd8's file before moving Ng3 to e4. This was playable, not a proven forced opening advantage.
- Nc3 checked Kb1 and attacked Rd1, but Qf3 could capture through e3/d3. After Qxc3, ...Rxd1 was answered by Rh1xd1 through empty g1/f1/e1. The liquidation traded rooks and won N; a checking fork does not guarantee profit. Verify the rear rook and all intermediate squares.
21.Ne4 Bf6 22.exf6 Qxf4 23.Bxf4 gxf6 24.Nxf6+ Kh8 25.Nd7+ Kg8 26.Qg3+ Kh8 27.Nxf8 e5 28.Bxh6 a6 29.Qg7#.
- Bf6 landed on e5's pawn capture square. Qxf4 removed the pawn but placed Q on Bc1-d2-e3-f4, a clear diagonal. Capture with the cheaper piece; destination safety fails even when the opponent describes activity or counterplay.
- Nf6 screened Qc3-d4-e5-f6-g7-h8. Nd7+ uncovered the queen check and attacked Rf8. After ...Kg8, K guarded f8; Qg3+ Kh8 relocated K before Nxf8, retaining the knight. Do not assume the king retreat was forced without checking alternatives.
- Bxh6 protected g7. Qg7# covered g8/h7 and could not be captured by Kh8; Nf8 also controlled h7. Verify the queen's path and all flights.
- Only dxe5 received a favorable White mark; only Nc3 was marked a Black blunder despite later direct losses. No invalid attempts; finished 14:11. Book mostly 3-6 seconds; Nd7 took 41, Nxf6 32, Bxh6 25. Shorten routine conversion after verifying guards. Black repeatedly spent 43-45 seconds without preventing tactical losses.

## T24 round 1: White Classical Caro, mate win

- dxe5 attacked Nf6 and cleared d4/d3 for Rd1. Nd7 was defended only by Qd8; the full exchange traded queens and won N. The opening was playable before the gift.
