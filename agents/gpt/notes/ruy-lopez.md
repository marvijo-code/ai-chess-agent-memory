# Ruy Lopez: guard deflection, passers and rook lines

## T44 SF2G1: White Chigorin vs Sonnet, mate win
- Nb3/Be3/Nbd2/Rc1/Bb1/Nf1-g3 developed smoothly. Qb7 released Nc6 before d5; Nb4/a3/Na6 followed. This game does not support an absolute ban on d5 after the pin disappears: calculate the resulting knight activity.
- Bd3 met Nc5. Bxc5 Rxc5 Rxc5 dxc5 Nxe5! won P by removing d6's guard of e5. Black's proposed Be7xc5 was blocked by its own d6 pawn. Bd6 then exposed Ne5 to Re8 and required Nxd7, removing a bishop while retaining the extra pawn. A favorable liquidation needs the final loose pawn identified before trading.
- Be5/Nc4 pressured b2. Qc1 defended it; Nc3 interrupted the bishop diagonal. Bd3 then cleared c2 so Qc1 could answer Bxc3? with Qxc3!, preserving the pawns. The retreat changed recapture geometry as well as attacking Nc4. After Ne5, f4! Nxd3 Qxd3! kept the extra pawn and removed the remaining minors.
- Qe3 guarded e4/f4; e5 and d6 created connected central passers. Qe4 offered a supported queen trade against Qd5; Rxe4 retained an active rook. Rd4 then stood behind d6 while Kf2-e3-e4 approached. Black's K reached e6: further pawn pushes needed a way to displace it.
- g4 prepared f5. Against f5+, exf6 en passant drew K to f6 and opened Kd5. The f-pawn was immediately recaptured; king access was the point. Include en passant when evaluating a pawn check and resulting king routes.
- Rc8 barred Kc6, but d7 attacked Rc8 and forced Rd8. Kc6 supported d7 AND vacated d5, clearing Rd4's file behind the passer. After Ke7, Kxb5 and Kxc4 removed queenside pawns. Rb8+/Rxb2 abandoned the blockade; d8=Q+ was protected by Rd4 through the now-empty d5/d6/d7 squares. Kxd8 was illegal. A king leaving the passer need not abandon its protection when a rook already covers promotion.
- Rd7+ forced Ke6; Qe7# used rook support, Kc4 and g4 against the flights. Promotion came with check, giving no time for counterplay.
- Zero invalid; Sonnet one (Ke7 into d6's attack). Finished 49:22 in 60+10. Nc3/Rd4/exf6 took 80/108/79 seconds; several ordinary improvements took 40-66. Spend critical time on changed guards and the break, then execute known rook placement and recaptures promptly.

## T44 round 4: White Open Ruy vs Sonnet
- Against Nxe4: d4/Bb3/dxe5/Nbd2/c3/Bc2. Nxb3 met axb3!, opening Ra1. bxc3?! after dxc3 allowed Qxd1!; no verified replacement stored.
- Rad8 abandoned a6. Rxa6 gained P, but Nb8/Ra7? was not a reliable invasion; Nc6? allowed Rxc7. Rxd1+ required Bxd1!; safe rook trades retained two extra pawns.
- Nc6 screened Bd7-b5 until Nxe5 cleared it; finish the bishop/knight exchange chain. K reached d7 before c6-c7, forcing Bxc7/Kxc7 and winning the last bishop.
- Bc5 and b4 guarded each other, tying enemy K down while mine took h7/g6. Do not push b5 into Kxb5. Bishop held the remote pawn while K attacked light-square pawns it could not capture.
- h8=Q allowed fxg2, but Qh5+ forced Kf4 and Qf5#. That exact mate justified ignoring the passer; promotion alone did not.
