- Same Zaitsev structure through Be3/Qd6. Nxe5 against Ne5 kept Qd1 guarding d5; Black's dxe5 was the correct recapture. Sparse marks do not establish an opening advantage.

- hxg6/fxg6 removed the pawn controlling f5 and emptied f7. Qd3 retained d5's guard while clearing d1; Qb3/Qxb3/Bxb3 removed queens with an extra knight.
- After Bxh6/Bxf8/Rxf8, d6+ cleared Bb3-c4-d5-e6-f7-g8. The pawn advance DISCOVERED bishop check; f7's earlier departure mattered. d7 was supported by Rd1 but could still fall to Bc6/Bxd7. Count every attacker, not just the rear rook.

- Black's rook took b2/f2/g2 while checking. Keep Ng3 safe before further gains; Ne2 answered Rxg2's attack. King activity and guarded minors outweighed these pawn losses, but this material margin is not permission for careless raids.
- Rc7/Rxc5 removed queenside counterplay. Bc6 cleared d5; Kd5 guarded e4. Rook checks required fresh destination scans. Re6 was bishop-supported; Na4 remained king-guarded when Rd4 attacked it, allowing Rxe5 before saving the knight.
- Nc5/Kc6 coordinated king and minors. Kc6 guarded Bd5/Nc5, freeing e5; Rd6 cleared e6 for the pawn. Kc7 cleared the d-file, Rd7+ and Kd6 restored escort. Ne4# checked Kf6: Rd7 covered the seventh rank, Kd6 covered e5/e6, Ne4 covered g5, and Black's g6/Rf5 occupied flights. Scan knight mate in queenless positions.
- No invalid moves; about 7:20 remained. Book mostly 4-9 seconds worked. Rxa6 took 55, Kc7 57; routine winning moves often took 18-34. Verified recaptures were faster. Enforce quiet-move caps throughout conversion. Sonnet made three invalid attempts on separate moves and used 2:13 on Kf6; these are not a winning plan.
