# Ruy Lopez: screens, pawn breaks and conversion

## Chigorin geometry
12.Nbd2 cxd4 13.cxd4 Nc6 14.d5 Nb4 15.Bb1 a5. Preserve Bc2 against ...Nb4/...Nxc2. ...a5 vacates a6 for Nb4-a6-c5; d6 supports Nc5. Ba2 reinforces d5 and frees Ra1. Trace recaptures and file screens.

## T15 semifinal 2 game 1: White vs Sonnet, mate win
16.Nf1 Bd7 17.Ng3 Rac8 18.a3 Na6 19.Ba2 Nc5 20.Be3?! Ncxe4 21.Nxe4 Nxe4! 22.Bb1 Nf6 23.Bg5 Nxd5?? 24.Qxd5 Bxg5 25.Nxg5.
- Be3 screened Re1 from e4. Ng3 alone could not preserve e4 against both knights; after the exchange Black won a pawn. Count usable defenders through all blockers. No best replacement for Be3 supplied.
- Bb1 attacked Ne4 through c2/d3. Bg5 cleared the e-file. ...Nxd5 overlooked Qd1's clear path to d5; the full capture chain left White an extra knight for two pawns.
26.Ne4 Bc6 27.Qxd6 Qxd6 28.Nxd6 Rcd8 29.Nf5 Rd7 30.Rxe5 Re8 31.Rxe8+ Kh7 32.Ne7+ g6 33.Nxc6.
- Qxd6 traded queens and recovered d6; Nxd6 attacked Rc8. Rxe5 recovered the other pawn, leaving a full knight ahead with equal pawns.
- Rd7 blocked Bc6-d7-e8, preventing ...Bxe8. The rook exchange therefore won a rook. Never assume a bishop can recapture through its own rook.
- Ne7+ was a discovered Bb1-h7 check: moving Nf5 cleared f5. It also attacked Bc6; ...g6 blocked the bishop check but allowed Nxc6.
- R1e3 supported Bd3, then R8e7 Rxe7 Rxe7 removed Black's last rook. Verified trades reduced counterplay.

### Two conversion errors despite ample clock
43.f4 f5 44.Re7+ Kf6 45.Rf7+ Ke6 46.Bc4+? bxc4 47.Nxc4? Kxf7.
- Black's b5 pawn could capture the checking bishop on c4. A check does not establish that the checking piece is safe or force the expected king move.
- Ne5 was Rf7's sole defender. Nxc4 removed it, allowing Ke6xf7. Re-evaluate lost guards after the opponent's unexpected capture; save threatened material before recapturing.
- Lost B+R within two moves despite no adverse viewer marks. Bc4 took 23 seconds, Nxc4 nine; over nine minutes remained. The win does not validate this sequence.

### Recovery through a supported passer
48.Nxa5 Ke6 49.Kf2 Kd5 50.Ke3 Kc5 51.b4+ Kb5 52.Kd4 Ka4 53.Nc4 Kb3 54.Kc5 Kc3 55.b5 Kb3 56.b6 g5 57.b7 gxf4 58.b8=Q+.
- b4 protected Na5; Kd4 then protected Nc4, which guarded a3. Kc5 supported the b-pawn, while Nc4 also guarded b6. Activate king and knight before pushing the majority past an active enemy king.
- Finish: ...Kc3 Qb2+ Kd3 Qd2+ Ke4 Qd5#. Nc4 protected Qb2; Ke3 protected Qd5. No illegal attempts; ended with about eight minutes.

## T15 round 3: White vs Sonnet, repetition draw
14.Nf1 Bd7 15.Be3 Rac8 16.d5?! Nb4 17.Bb1 a5?! 18.a3 Nc2 19.Bxc2 Qxc2! 20.Qxc2 Rxc2! 21.Rab1?! a4 22.Ng3 Rfc8 23.Rec1 Rxc1+ 24.Rxc1 Rxc1+ 25.Bxc1.
- Nc2 attacks Re1/Be3, not Qd1. Full liquidation leaves equal material and an active Black rook. d5/Rab1 were inaccurate; no best alternatives supplied.
- b3 axb3 Nxb3 exchanged b-pawn for a-pawn. Later a4? bxa4 lost the remaining queenside pawn; Bd7 protected a4.
- Kb4 Ng4 f3 Ne3 Ndf1 Nxg2: an attacked knight captured instead of retreating. Nf5+ Bxf5 exf5 Nf4 forked d5/h5; Ne3 did not guard h5.
- Kc6/Nc4 pressured d6, but ...Nb4+ displaced d5's king defender, then ...Nxd5+ took it. Repetition escaped three pawns down; not proof it was forced.
