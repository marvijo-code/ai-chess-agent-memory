## T44 round 4: White Open Ruy vs Sonnet, mate win
- Black chose Nxe4, b5/d5/Be6. My d4/Bb3/dxe5/Nbd2/c3/Bc2 met Nc5 and d4. Against Nxb3, axb3! preserved bishops and opened Ra1. bxc3?! after dxc3 allowed Qxd1!; Rxd1 was legal, but the pawn recapture was marked inaccurate. No verified replacement supplied.
- Rad8 abandoned a6. Rxa6 took it and attacked Nc6. Nb8?! kicked R, but Ra7? was a mistake; do not store this as a reliable invasion. Nc6? then allowed Rxc7. Rxd1+ required Bxd1!, preserving two extra pawns. Rc8 was answered by Rxc8+/Bxc8, removing all rooks and counterplay.
- Nd4/Nxb5 invited Nxe5; Bxe5/Bxb5 exchanged both knights and left my connected b/c passers, two extra pawns and bishop pair. Bd7 did not guard b5 while Nc6 blocked its diagonal; Nxe5 cleared the path. Calculate through that final capture.
- c4 gained a bishop tempo. Kf1-e2-d2-c3 supported c5 and the queenside. Bxc6+/Kxc6 exchanged light bishops. Kb4-b5-c6-d7 then cleared c6 and guarded c7. The c-pawn forced Bxc7/Kxc7, winning Black's last bishop. A king in front of passers can remove the blockade before pushing.
- After b4/Kc4, b5 would lose to Kxb5. My note proposed Bd2; actual Bc5 also worked: b4 guarded Bc5 and Bc5 guarded b4. This mutual protection survived Kb5 and freed K for the other wing. Verify BOTH guards and bishop safety before leaving.
- Black put f5/g6/h7 on light squares beyond my dark bishop. Kf6-g7 took the h7 base, then g6; h-pawn advanced under king protection. A bishop unable to attack a pawn chain can still hold the remote passer while K attacks its base.
- Note at h4 said answer f3 with gxf3. I instead promoted after f3 and allowed fxg2, but Qh5+ forced Kf4 and Qf5# followed. This was a verified forcing finish, not permission to ignore enemy passers. Compare removing f3 first; promotion alone does not settle the race.
- Zero invalid; finished 54:52 in 60+10. Rxa6/Ra7/Bc5 took 54/39/45 seconds; Bxd1 took three. Spend critical time on captures, guards and blockade routes, then execute verified conversion promptly.
