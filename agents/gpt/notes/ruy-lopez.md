- e5 was the only good reply to ...d5. Calculate the liquidation through Bxe4 rather than treating the pawn advance as merely a knight chase.
- Be3 defended d4. Qe2 later removed Qd1 from behind d4 on the contested d-file; Red1 and Rc2-d2 reinforced the center. The result and sparse marks do not prove every quiet move was best.

### Blocked recapture wins queen
After 20...Rd5 21.Rc1 Qe6 22.Qe2 Be7 23.Red1 Bf8 24.Qc4 Qd7 25.Bf4 Rd8 26.Be3 Be7 27.Rc2 Bf8 28.Rcd2 Na5 29.Qd3 Nc6 30.Qe4 Qf5?? 31.Qxf5:
- Black had rooks d5/d8 and my pawn on e5. Rd5 could not recapture on f5 through e5; Black's queen was undefended. Trace the entire proposed recapture path before accepting or offering a queen trade.
- 31...g6 32.Qg4 Bg7 33.e6 fxe6 34.Qxe6+ Kh8 35.Qxg6 Ne7 36.Qf7 Nf5: e5 was now empty, so Rd5 genuinely defended Nf5. Qxf5 would lose queen for knight. A blocked line can become live after my own pawn moves.

### Successive forks and mating coordination
37.Qxc7 R8d7 38.Qf4 Nxe3 39.Qxe3 Rd8 40.Ne5 Rf8 41.Ng6+ Kg8 42.Ne7+ Kh7 43.Nxd5.
- Ng6+ forked Kh8/Rf8. Instead of Nxf8 followed by a bishop recapture, Ne7+ forked Kg8/Rd5; the played continuation won the rook without surrendering the knight. Check for a second safe checking fork before collecting the first target.
- 43...Rf7 44.Qe4+ Kg8 45.Re2 Bf6 46.Nxf6+ Rxf6 47.Qe8+ Kh7 48.Re7+ Rf7 49.Qxf7+ Kh8 50.Qh7#. Re7 protected the final queen on h7 through the cleared seventh rank.
- No invalid attempts; finished with 8:48. Familiar development banked time, but Rc2 took 53 seconds and Nxf6+ took 55 in a won position. Use brief safety checks for verified simplification and coordinated mate.

## T12 round 1: Black vs Sonnet, repetition draw
...Qd7/Rad8/d4 cxd4 Nxd4 Nxd4 exd4 Nf5 g6 Nh6+ Bxh6 Bxh6 Nxe4? dxe4 d3 Bxd3?? Qxd3! Qxd3 Rxd3!.
- ...Nxe4's pawn-fork justification was unsound; White's Bxd3 let Black recover. Calculate queen escapes and counterthreats before claiming a fork recovers a sacrifice. The liquidation left equal 2R+B and six pawns.
- c4 defended Rd3. Rxd3 cxd3 created a passer but removed that rook. Rd1/Ke3 then supplied two attackers against Rd7's single defense; Ke6 did not defend d3. Rxd3 Rxd3+ Kxd3 removed the passer.
- Opposite-colored bishops, White six pawns against five: ...f5 exchanged the central e-pawn, ...h5 fixed the kingside, and ...Kg4 activated the king. Final Kd6 with Bd5/Bc6 barred c5/e5 and preserved b5. The barrier held by repetition; this does not prove all earlier positions drawn.

## Earlier recurring tactics
- T11 Sonnet: Ke5/Rd8/Bd5 against Nd4/Rd1 allowed Nc6+; Bxc6 vacated d5 for Rxd8. Two misses did not validate the king move.
- ...f2 opened Bd5-e4-f3-g2-h1 to protect promotion. ...Ke1 cleared f1 for another queen.
- Rc1/Qc7 can have two screens, Bc2/Nc5. ...Na4 Bxa4 bxa4 Rxc7 removed both. Be3 Nxd4 Nxd4 exd4 Qxd4 abandoned Bc2 to Qxc2.
- f3-f4, Re3-g3, and Nh4-f5/Bxf5/Ng3xf5 remove e4 defenders. Be7 can block Re8's defense of e5.
- Bxd7 Nxd7 can replace one knight with another, retaining ...Nxf8. Track both knights and new defenders.
- Bg5 allowed fxg5 when f6 was unpinned; Qa4 allowed bxa4; Nb3 allowed axb3. Useful plans do not excuse unsafe destinations.
