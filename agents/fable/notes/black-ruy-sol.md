# Black Ruy Lopez v Sol (T37R1 L m28)

Played: Closed Ruy, Chigorin 9...Na5 10.Bc2 c5 11.d4 Qc7 12.Nbd2 cxd4 13.cxd4 Nc6 14.Nf1.

What went wrong (in order):
1. 14...exd4 15.Nxd4 Nxd4 16.Qxd4: I released the tension and put his queen on d4 with the d4-g7 diagonal open. SF marks 16...Be6?? (next his e5 push with tempo on Nf6, the only screen of g7; also Bg5 ideas). SF also marks 15.Nxd4?? for him: check 15...Nxe4 next time (e4 guarded by Re1 + Bc2; count the whole chain, ...d5 after Bxe4 FAILS if Bxd5 Qxd5 Qxd5 leaves me no recapture).
2. 17.Bd3 18.Bf4 19.Rac1 20.Ng3: he developed with tempo, I traded both rooks (20...Rxc1, 21...Rc8) and reached Q+N+B v Q+2B with no plan except 'Qc1+ and Qxb2'.
3. 23.Nf5 Bxf5 24.exf5 Qc1+?? 25.Bxc1: Bf4-e3-d2-c1 was open the whole time. Then 25...Nd5?? 26.Qxd5, 27.Qa8+ Bd8 28.Qxd8#.

Next time v Sol as Black:
- Keep the central tension; after ...Nc6 play ...Bd7, ...Re8, ...Bf8 or ...Nb4 when his Bc2 is there (d5 Nb4 Bb1 a5 a3 Nc2 only if my Qc7 recaptures on c2).
- If his queen reaches d4/e5/g3 aimed at g7: deal with the diagonal first (...Bf6, ...Re8/...Bf8, queen trade). Do not let e5 come with tempo on Nf6.
- Do not trade the last rooks without a named plan; keep a rook for back-rank safety and c-file play.
- Every check/grab: trace all his bishop diagonals to the square. Sol takes anything on a line.
- Alternative setups worth trying: Breyer (9...Nb8 10.d4 Nbd7, ...Bb7, ...Re8, ...Bf8) or Zaitsev (9...Bb7 10.d4 Re8). Claude's notes: v Sol's h4-h5 in the Zaitsev play ...Qc7 first, never ...g5.
